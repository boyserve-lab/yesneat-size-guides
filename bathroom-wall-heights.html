Bathroom wall heights: mirror, sconce, towel bar, hook

Every bathroom wall fixture has one height that works, and it is derived from body height rather than from the fixture. Get the derivation and the numbers stop being a matter of taste.

The anchor for all of it is eye level: 60 to 62 inches for an average adult, which is the mirror's center, not its top. Everything else is measured from a body position: standing, reaching, seated, or stepping out of a tub.

Grab bar heights follow access standards rather than taste: 33 to 36 inches above the finished floor is the band that works for both seated and standing transfer.

Why the mirror is 60 to 62

A mirror centered at eye level shows the head and shoulders without forcing a bend. Centered at 60 inches, a 30 inch tall mirror spans 45 to 75 inches, which covers the countertop backsplash to a few inches above the head of a 5 foot 10 adult. Centered at 62, it moves up 2 inches and clips the backsplash instead. That is the whole argument, and it is why a 36 inch mirror over a 36 inch vanity reads correctly while the same mirror over a 30 inch vanity floats.

Sconces beside or above the mirror

Sconces go beside the mirror at 66 to 72 inches, or above it at 75 to 80. Beside is better: light at eye level and from the side gives an even face, while above-cabinet light casts shadows down the cheeks. The number that decides is the mirror top: if the mirror top is at 72, sconces beside it sit at 66 to 72 center, about 8 to 12 inches out from the glass edge.

Towel bars and how far they stick out

A towel bar at 48 inches center is reachable without a bend and clear of a 36 inch vanity top. Depth matters as much as height: a bar with 3 to 4 inches of projection holds a folded bath towel without touching the wall, and anything under 2.5 inches leaves the towel flat against the paint, which is why it never dries. Ring and bar both project 2.5 to 4 inches; hooks project 2 to 3.

The tub and shower side

A shower head outlet sits at 78 to 82 inches, with 80 as the common rough-in, measured from the finished floor. The valve trim centers at 36 to 42, with 40 as the typical, which puts it below the shoulder of a standing adult and above the tub rim. In a tub-and-shower combination with a 60 inch tub, the towel bar usually goes on the wall opposite the shower at 48 inches center, which is high enough to clear the tub apron and low enough to reach from outside.

Children and the 42 inch line

Everything a child reaches should sit at 42 inches or below: hand towel bar, hooks at the child's height, and the bottom of the mirror if it is shared. The usual compromise is a lower hook beside the tub at 42 and a second hook at 60 for adults, which costs one extra hook and removes the "towel on the floor" problem entirely.
