Curtain panel sizes in inches: width, length and rod rules

Curtains are sold by panel width and finished length. One number decides fullness, the other decides whether the panel touches the floor.

A curtain panel is not a window measurement. Panels are flat widths that get pleated onto a rod, so a 50 inch window does not take a 50 inch panel. Plan on two panels per window at 1.5 to 2 times the window width in total flat fabric.

Fullness matters more than the width number: 1.5x the rod width looks flat, 2x looks gathered. A 72 inch rod with two 52 inch panels gives 104 inches of fabric, or 1.4x, which reads as barely pleated.

The rod height calculation

Mount the rod 4 to 6 inches above the window frame, or at the ceiling line for a taller look. Then: rod height minus 0.5 inch equals the panel length for a break, and rod height plus 1 to 2 inches equals the length for a puddle. A rod at 92 inches on a wall takes a 95 inch panel for a break and a 108 inch panel for a puddle.

Rod width and returns

The rod runs 4 to 8 inches wider than the window on each side, so panels stack over the wall and not over the glass. A 48 inch window wants a 56 to 64 inch rod. Double rods need 5 to 6 inches of wall between them, and a return to the wall on each end stops light from leaking around the edge.

What breaks a curtain plan

Radiators and deep sills: a panel needs 2 inches of clearance to hang free, so measure from the wall, not the frame. Tile floors: a puddle drags, so use a break. Blackout liners add 1 to 2 inches of bulk at the top, which eats rod clearance and can bind at the brackets.
