Dining table size and chair clearance in inches

The table is the easy number. The hard number is the 36 inches a chair needs behind it, which is what actually decides whether your dining room seats six or four.

A dining table has two dimensions that matter: the surface, and the clear floor around it. Buy from the surface and the room fills up; buy from the clearance and the table ends up smaller than expected, which is usually the right call.

Allow 24 inches of table edge per person, and 30 inches if the table is the only place anyone sits. A round table of 48 inches seats four, of 60 inches seats six.

Chair pull-out clearance

36 inches of clear floor behind the table edge is the working number: 18 inches to slide the chair out from under, plus 18 inches to walk behind it. 30 inches works for a table against a wall where nobody walks behind. 42 inches is comfortable for a table used every day.

Read backwards: a 96 inch wall with a 36 inch table needs 96 minus 36 minus 72 equals minus 12 inches. The table does not fit with chairs on both long sides against a wall; it fits with one bench against the wall, which returns the 36 inches on that side.

Rug under the table

A rug has to extend 24 inches past the table edge on all sides, because that is where the chair lands when pulled out. A 40 x 72 table therefore wants at least a 7 x 11 ft rug, and most 8 x 10 rugs are 16 inches short on the long axis.

Pendant and headroom

A pendant hangs 30 to 36 inches above the table surface, and no lower than 60 inches from the floor, which keeps it out of eye line when seated. For an 8 ft ceiling that leaves 96 minus 60 equals 36 inches of drop from ceiling to table, which is exactly the working window.
