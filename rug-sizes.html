Rug sizes in feet and inches, room by room

Rugs are sold in feet and measured into inches once the room is real. Convert first, then check how much bare floor is left at each side.

A rug is a floor object, not a wall object, and the whole decision is how much floor it may cover. In a small room the answer is usually one size smaller than the room suggests; in a large room it is two sizes larger than the sofa suggests.

Rug dimensions are nominal and run 1 to 3 inches short in each direction. A pad should sit 2 inches inside the rug edge on every side, or the rug curls.

The bare-floor rules

Leave 4 to 6 inches of bare floor between a rug edge and the wall. In a hallway, leave 3 to 6 inches on each side of the runner so doors and shoes have somewhere to go. Under a dining table, the rug has to extend 24 inches past the table edge on all four sides, because that is how much floor a chair needs to pull out.

Read backwards, those rules size the rug: a 5 x 8 ft room holds a 4 x 7 rug at best, and a 2 x 6 runner in a 36 inch hallway leaves 3 inches on each side, which is correct and looks right.

Rug under a bed

A 6 x 9 rug under a Queen bed, 60 x 80 in, extends 24 inches past the mattress on each side and 24 inches past the foot: 60 plus 48 is 108 in wide, which is why 6 x 9 is the practical minimum for nightstands. An 8 x 10 is the same layout with 30 inches per side. A 5 x 8 only works as a three-quarter rug, starting at the foot and running out into the room.

Pile and door clearance

A rug thicker than 0.5 inch blocks most interior doors, which have 0.75 inch of clearance under the leaf. In a bathroom or a hallway, keep pile under 0.4 inch, or choose a flatweave. Bath mats at 17 x 24 or 20 x 30 in work because they sit inside the door swing; see the bath mat and bed frame numbers for the doorway math.
