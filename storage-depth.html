Closet and cabinet depth in inches: 12, 15, 18, 24

Cabinet depth is the single number that decides what a closet can hold. Twelve inches holds shoes; twenty-four holds a hanging garment. Everything in between is a compromise, and the compromise is usually a door that does not close.

Depths are measured from the back panel to the front of the cabinet carcass, excluding the door. The usable interior is that number minus the thickness of the door and the depth of the hinges, which costs 1 inch on a standard overlay and 2 inches on a full-overlay with a thicker panel.

24 in22 inHanging garments, suitcases, storage bins stacked two deep

23.5 to 24 inches is the standard wardrobe depth in the US, and it exists because a men's shirt on a hanger is 17 to 18 inches wide on the shoulder and needs clearance to swing.

Hanger width and the 24 inch rule

A shirt on a hanger occupies 17 to 18 inches across the shoulders. A suit jacket occupies 18 to 19. A winter coat occupies 20 to 22. Add 2 inches of clearance to swing the hanger, and the interior has to be 20 to 24 inches deep, which is why 24 inch cabinets are the hanging standard and 18 inch cabinets are described as "reach-in" and used for shelving only.

Sliding doors cost depth

A hinged door costs 1 inch of interior depth and 20 inches of swing space in front of the cabinet. A sliding door costs 4 inches of interior depth because the panels ride in front of each other, but zero swing space. In a closet 24 inches deep, a sliding door leaves 20 inches of interior, which is under the hanging minimum, so closets with sliders are usually built 30 inches deep.

The 27 gallon tote does not fit an 18 inch deep cabinet: 18 inches of depth against a 30 inch bin length only works if the bin is turned sideways, and then the 14 inch height overruns a 12 inch shelf gap. This is the exact place where depth, width and shelf height have to be checked together rather than one at a time.

Shoe storage, worked backwards

An adult men's shoe is about 11.5 inches long, a women's about 10.5, a boot 12 to 14. A 12 inch cabinet holds shoes face-in with 0.5 inch of clearance, which is why 12 inch shoe cabinets are often sold with angled shelves. A 15 inch cabinet holds them flat with room for the door to close. Anything under 12 inches holds only slippers, sandals and children's shoes, which is a real category and the reason "shoe cabinet" listings range from 9 to 15 inches.
